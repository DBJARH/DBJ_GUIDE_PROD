    Taxonomy Usage Guide

    How to give a Product its place in the DBJ Taxonomy, and how to keep a shipping log beside it.
    Version 0.1

    Written against the DBJ Taxonomy Core, version 0.1. The Core defines the names. This page shows how to use them.

    What you are doing#

    You give every Product one place in the Taxonomy. A Product is whatever the organization ships: a software product, a service, documentation, a physical book.

    The place is written as a plaque: Category.Capability.

    Physical.Compute
Logical.Integration
Implementation.Deployment

    That is all the Taxonomy asks. It never says what a Product is or what the organization produces. The business declares that.

    The tree#

    ├── Conceptual
│   ├── Business
│   ├── Information
│   ├── Application
│   └── Technology
├── Logical
│   ├── Data Management
│   ├── Integration
│   ├── Platform
│   └── Security
├── Physical
│   ├── Compute
│   ├── Infrastructure
│   ├── Network
│   └── Storage
└── Implementation
    ├── Deployment
    ├── Development
    ├── Monitoring
    └── Operations

    Four Categories, four Capabilities each. Sixteen places in all.

    How to write a plaque#

    1. Choose the Category. Ask what the Product describes. What and why: Conceptual. How it is structured: Logical. What it runs on: Physical. How it is built and operated: Implementation.
    2. Choose the Capability inside that Category.
    3. Write it as Category.Capability. Use the Core’s names exactly.

    If a Product seems to belong in two places, it is usually two Products. Split it.

    Illustrative only. The rows are an organization’s own, the tree is not.

    The shipping log#

    A shipping container carries its marking on the side, and a manifest travels with it. The plaque is the marking. The shipping log is the manifest: whatever else the organization wants to know about the Product.

    The log is optional, and each organization decides how much goes into it.

    • Minimal: the plaque and an owner.
    • Fuller: the Business, Product or Technology segment, the lifecycle stage, tags.

    Three rules:

    1. The log never moves a Product. A book in a library can carry any number of tags. Its shelf does not depend on any of them.
    2. The organization owns the values. Lifecycle stages in particular are private to the organizational unit. The Taxonomy does not define them.
    3. A tag never redefines a Taxonomy name. Do not use Security for something other than Logical.Security.

    Common mistakes#

    • Synonyms. The names are official vocabulary. Do not rename or reinterpret them.
    • Mixed Categories. Do not put concerns from different Categories in one section.
    • Tags as places. If a tag decides where a Product lives, it has become a second taxonomy. Remove it.
    • Starting complex. Begin with the plaque. Add log fields only when someone needs them.
